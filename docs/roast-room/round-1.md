# Round 1: opening positions

## Hormozi

SEAT: Hormozi (Offers, pricing, and sales)
STANCE: Strong yes
OPTION SCORES: A 8/10 · B 4/10 · C 1/10
PILLAR SCORE: 8/10
CONFIDENCE: 4/5
RUIN RISK: none
DOOR: two-way

THE CALL: A, narrowed to one avatar (business owners before and after a sale), with the hero line stating that outcome in plain words and families shown second.

WHY:
1. The constraint is leads, not unit economics (Constraint first, S113). My assumption: a $1.5M owner at about 1.0% pays $15K a year. Kitces puts niche referral cost at $0.06 vs $0.13 per $1 of new revenue, so CAC falls from about $1,950 to $900. Lifetime gross profit is roughly $127K (assuming 85% margin and 10 years). That clears even the 12:1 floor for businesses where people staff every step (LTV:CAC, S089). "You also will reduce CAC because your messaging is now aligned with that avatar." (S094)
2. Customers are fractal 80/20 (Price anchors, S021). $7.5M is 5 owners at $1.5M or 25 families at $300K (both balances assumed). On about 40 year-one consults, that is a 13% close rate versus 63%. A makes the goal reachable.
3. "Comprehensive. Holistic. Personal." moves nothing in the Value Equation (S115), so buyers compare your 1.50% to the next adviser's. That is the bad-offer symptom (Grand Slam Offer, S114). Three equal doors means three avatars at $0 AUM, which breaks the 111 rule (S059). Illinois bans testimonials, so Jake's deal background is your proof. C hides fees and location: "Nothing sells like the truth." (S023)

THE RISK I SEE: Owners book the consult but only move money at the liquidity event, so the 12-month AUM target slips even with the right positioning.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND: If fewer than 1 in 4 names on Jake's warm list are owners with $1M+ investable, the avatar is wrong and B with families first wins.

WHAT I'D DO THIS WEEK: Jake lists 100 owners, CPAs and M&A attorneys from his banking years, books 10 conversations, and notes which homepage line they repeat back (Rule of 100, S002).

QUESTION FOR THE FOUNDER: Name your first ten clients: how many own a business, and what is their average investable balance?

## Gary V

SEAT: Gary V (Marketing and attention)
STANCE: Yes
OPTION SCORES: A 8/10 · B 3/10 · C 1/10
PILLAR SCORE: 5/10
CONFIDENCE: 4/5
RUIN RISK: none
DOOR: two-way

THE CALL: Build A for business owners. The site is only where people land. Jake's deal-table story on LinkedIn is what brings them there.

WHY:
1. A website does not create attention. "Before you can sell anything, you need somebody's attention." (S011, 2020). My assumed math: $5M at a $500K average account is 10 clients. At a 25% close rate that is 40 consults, about 3 to 4 a month. The site turns that traffic into calls. It does not bring the traffic.
2. Baskin Robbins, not vanilla (S026). Every adviser in Chicago already says "Comprehensive. Holistic. Personal." That is vanilla. A has two shores, two advisors and one real story. "In fact, one of the most important things for a lot of you is to go even more niche." (S047, 2026). Kitces agrees: firms with a niche on the site get referrals at $0.06 versus $0.13. That is half the price.
3. Day Trading Attention (S096, S145). Business owners are on LinkedIn today, and it is cheap attention for a firm this small. "You own LinkedIn and you don't do it." (S043, 2026). My read on Kitces scoring social media worst: most advisers post bland content, then blame the channel (S030). And with 62% of new clients coming from referrals, A makes it easy for a CPA to forward. B does not.

THE RISK I SEE: 2 to 4 hours a month is one post. "Don't have time? Don't get results." (S005, 2019). A niche site with no distribution is a billboard in the desert.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND: Twelve weeks of posting. If the family-planning posts beat the business-owner posts 2 to 1 on views and DMs, the audience has voted, and the homepage leads with families.

WHAT I'D DO THIS WEEK: Document, don't create (S143, 2016). Jake records one 20-minute voice memo on "what happens to an owner's money the day after the sale." Cut it into 10 LinkedIn posts, get each one through compliance review, and post the first by Friday.

QUESTION FOR THE FOUNDER: Will you put your own face and voice on LinkedIn three times a week for a year?

## Graham

SEAT: Graham (Product and startups)
STANCE: Yes
OPTION SCORES: A 8/10 · B 4/10 · C 2/10
PILLAR SCORE: 6/10
CONFIDENCE: 3/5
RUIN RISK: none
DOOR: two-way

THE CALL: A, but narrower: lead with Chicago and West Michigan owners who are selling a business or just sold one. The lake map is decoration, not the pitch.

WHY:
1. Narrow beats broad (Small intense fire, S010). "A need that's narrow but genuine is a better starting point than one that's broad but hypothetical." (S174) Assume a post-sale owner brings $2M and a family brings $300K. Then the $5M target means 3 owners or 17 families. Two advisors can find 3 owners by hand. B's "Comprehensive. Holistic. Personal." is on every adviser's homepage.
2. The site isn't the bottleneck. Referrals are (Find the bottleneck, S010). 62% of new clients come from referrals. Booking 3 to 5 consults a month from the site at an assumed 1 to 2% conversion needs 150 to 500 qualified visitors a month, and 2 to 4 hours of content a month won't bring them. So the site's job is to convert referrals. That's why a stated niche ($0.06 vs $0.13 per $1 of new revenue, a link and not proof) and posted fees matter. C fails at this.
3. Do it by hand first (Do things that don't scale, S096). "Who wants this so much that they'll use it even when it's a crappy version one made by a two-person startup they've never heard of?" (S098) That describes SWM. The answer is an owner at the point of selling, found through the lawyers and CPAs who see the sale first. This is a services firm, not a startup, so I don't want 5% weekly growth. I want a specific user.

THE RISK I SEE: Playing house (S008, S092, S178). They build a handsome site while nobody calls owners, and month 6 brings no consults.
WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND: If Jake can't name 20 owners or referral sources who would take his call, or most of the first 10 consults are families under $500K, switch to leading with Adam's families.
WHAT I'D DO THIS WEEK: Jake lists 30 M&A attorneys, CPAs and business brokers across Chicago and West Michigan and calls ten of them before the site exists.
QUESTION FOR THE FOUNDER: Fifteen months in at $0 AUM: who were your serious prospects, and why didn't any of them move money?

## Buffett

SEAT: Buffett (Money and capital)
STANCE: Conditional yes
OPTION SCORES: A 7/10 · B 5/10 · C 2/10
PILLAR SCORE: 7/10
CONFIDENCE: 3/5
RUIN RISK: none
DOOR: two-way

THE CALL: Lead with A, but only after a compliance review of every claim, with Jake's insurance pay shown right next to the fee calculator.

WHY:
1. At 1.50% on passive index funds, SWM will never be the low-cost operator, so it needs the niche: "only a very low-cost operator or someone operating in a protected, and usually small, niche can sustain high profitability levels." (Franchise versus commodity, S060). Every adviser in Chicago already says "Comprehensive. Holistic. Personal."
2. Here's the cash math (Birds in the bush, S011). My assumptions: 3 to 5 consults a month and one in three closes. That gives 6 to 10 clients in six months. At $500K each, that's $3M to $5M, the bottom of the target. One owner with $2M at an assumed 1% pays $20K a year, close to what three $500K families pay at 1.5% ($22.5K). The 2 to 4 content hours are the same, and the birds are bigger.
3. C is a mistake of omission (Mistakes of omission, S028). 62% of new clients come from referrals, and a one-page site with no fees and no address loses referrals we never see. My reflex is to sit still. That reflex misfires for a firm starting from $0.

THE RISK I SEE: The front-page story writes itself: "New adviser courts business owners with Wall Street pitch, sells them insurance on commission." Business owners are the natural buyers of buy-sell and key-person policies, and "Don’t ask the barber whether you need a haircut." (S082) Stretch an analyst's resume into "the deal table" and a firm with no track record has nothing to cushion the hit. "We can afford to lose money but we can't afford to lose reputation." (S028)

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND: If insurance commissions are planned at more than about 20% of first-year revenue, I move to B, or to A with commissions credited against advisory fees.

WHAT I'D DO THIS WEEK: Get a state-RIA compliance reviewer to check four things before the builder designs anything: the hero line against Jake's actual deal record, the fee calculator at every breakpoint, anything that reads like a testimonial under Illinois rules, and any mention of Texas before that registration clears.

QUESTION FOR THE FOUNDER: Jake, what share of your first-year income do you expect from insurance commissions, and will you put that number on the fee page?
