Who we work with

Most people call us at a turning point: a sale, a new job, a retirement date. Here's what we'd work on with you, and an example of how it plays out.

Check first · R7

The examples below are labelled as made-up clients. Check that Illinois rules allow that, or leave it to Jake's reviewer.

Business owners

Your company is probably your biggest asset, and it rarely gets planned alongside the rest of your money. We look at both, with your CPA and attorney.

  1. Work out what you need from a sale to retire, years before buyers call.
  2. Set a salary and a retirement plan that suit you and your team.
  3. Keep business risk away from family money, and review buy-sell and key-person cover.
  4. Have a plan for the proceeds before the deal closes.

Jake is a licensed insurance agent and may earn commissions. You never have to buy insurance through us.

Example client, not a real person

Maria, 56, owns a commercial cleaning company near Grand Rapids.

She has 40 employees and expects offers within five years. She wants to know whether a sale would pay for her retirement, and what to do first.

We'd work out the after-tax amount she needs from a sale, set up a retirement plan that helps her and her staff now, coordinate with her CPA and attorney on the deal structure, and plan how to invest the proceeds.

Professionals

Rising pay brings more choices: stock awards, bonuses, old 401(k)s, a bigger house, college. We put them in one plan so they stop competing.

  1. Decide what to do with old 401(k)s.
  2. Understand how RSUs, bonuses and deferred pay are taxed, and when to sell.
  3. Save for a home and for college at the same time.
  4. Invest at low cost, with taxes in mind.

Read: your 401(k) options when you change jobs

Example clients, not real people

Dev and Priya, 38 and 36, live in Chicago with two kids.

Between them they have four old 401(k)s, a yearly stock grant and a goal of a bigger house in three years.

We'd combine the old accounts where it lowers cost, set a plan for each stock grant as it vests, and line up savings for the house and for college.

Families near retirement

The years either side of retirement hold the biggest one-time money decisions most families make. We help you make them in the right order, with numbers.

  1. Turn savings into a monthly income.
  2. Decide when to claim Social Security, and whether to take a pension as a lump sum.
  3. Update beneficiaries, estate documents and gifts with your attorney.
  4. Leave one clear picture your family can follow.

Example clients, not real people

Tom and Linda, 62 and 59, live near Kalamazoo.

Tom has to choose between a pension lump sum and monthly payments. They aren't sure when to claim Social Security.

We'd compare both pension choices in dollars, model different claiming ages for each of them, and build a withdrawal plan that turns their savings into a steady income.
